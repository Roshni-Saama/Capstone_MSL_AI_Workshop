## The manager brief

The original brief is in `03_MSL_KOL_Engagement_Agent.docx`.

The challenge is intentionally open-ended. The team should research current work, identify users and data sources, define a focused problem, develop an end-to-end workflow, design the experience, and explain where agents create value. Guardrails, transparency, governance, and human oversight form part of the solution.

Possible areas include KOL briefings, relevant scientific discussion topics, post-visit insight capture, routing questions or potential safety information, and identifying patterns across engagements. These are options, not a requirement to implement every feature.

The brief is business-led, with technical and governance deliverables. It does not prescribe an architecture, model, or platform. Its description of notebook-based work is a starting scenario, not evidence that every MSL team operates that way.

## What open ended means

Interpretation: choose a focused problem within MSL and KOL engagement and justify the value of solving it. The suggested features are possible directions, not a requirement to implement all of them. Preparation and follow-up assistance remains a proposed scope.

## Choosing a first problem

Proposed question: How might we help an MSL prepare a source-backed KOL briefing and track scientific questions through reviewed follow-up?

Validate whether preparation effort or follow-up closure is the stronger problem. Confirm available data and a demonstrable prototype boundary before selecting the scope.

## Open questions for the manager or MSL

- Which engagement stage creates the most difficulty, and can they give an example?
- Which records and scientific materials can the project use?
- Who can validate the workflow and review outputs?
- What improvement would be useful, and how can we measure a baseline?
- Which company procedures govern review, privacy, and routing?
