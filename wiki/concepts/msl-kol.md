# MSL and KOL concepts

Status: draft revised for user review on 13 October 2026. Owner: [To complete]. Stakeholder reviewer and review date: [To complete].

A Medical Science Liaison connects a pharma company's scientific knowledge with the questions arising in clinical practice. Understanding this role requires both the reporting structure inside the company and the two-way scientific exchange with external experts.

## Definitions

| Concept | Definition to validate | Supporting source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medical Science Liaison (MSL) | A scientific professional within Medical Affairs who conducts balanced scientific exchange with healthcare professionals, researchers, and KOLs, brings field questions and insights into the company, and supports evidence generation. | [Role guidance](../../raw/MSL%20role%20and%20reporting%20research.md#role-and-relationships); [discussion notes](../../raw/MSL%20KOL%20engagement%20note.md#the-msl-role-and-value) |
| Key Opinion Leader (KOL) | A clinician or researcher recognised for expertise in a clinical or scientific field. For this project, an external scientific stakeholder whose questions and perspectives inform the engagement. | [Discussion notes](../../raw/MSL%20KOL%20engagement%20note.md#the-msl-role-and-value); company identification criteria to validate |
| Scientific engagement | A purposeful, two-way discussion of scientific evidence, its limitations, and unmet information needs. The discussion has a scientific objective and requires accurate, balanced information. | [Role guidance](../../raw/MSL%20role%20and%20reporting%20research.md#role-and-relationships) |
| Observation versus insight | An observation records what was said or seen. A potential insight explains why it may matter and what question or action it could inform; interpretation requires validation. | [Illustrative documentation example](../../raw/MSL%20KOL%20engagement%20note.md#documentation); [insights process](../../raw/MSL%20scientific%20training%20and%20medical%20insights%20research.md#medical-insights-process) |

## MSL structure

### Position within the company

Professional guidance recommends that the MSL function report into Medical Affairs and remain independent of sales targets. [Role and reporting evidence](../../raw/MSL%20role%20and%20reporting%20research.md#role-and-relationships).

An illustrative reporting structure in a pharma company is:

```text
Pharma company
    |
Medical Affairs leadership
    |
Field Medical or MSL team leadership
    |
Medical Science Liaison
```

Titles and management layers vary. This chart gives us roles to investigate; the actual reporting line and approval authority need company confirmation.

### Scientific exchange and collaboration

The MSL also maintains working relationships with external experts and internal teams. These relationships should be considered separately from the reporting hierarchy.

```text
Company scientific evidence --> MSL --> Clinical community
Company medical teams      <-- MSL <-- Questions and field observations
```

| Relationship | Purpose to understand | What needs validation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medical Affairs or Field Medical manager | Sets direction and provides team oversight | Actual reporting line, responsibilities, and review authority |
| KOLs, clinicians, and researchers | Exchange evidence and clarify scientific questions | Scientific objectives and engagement needs |
| Medical-information colleagues | Help address evidence questions and prepare reviewed responses | Company response process and owners |
| Medical strategy or research colleagues | Assess potential insights and evidence gaps | Insight review and research decision authority |
| Pharmacovigilance or drug safety | Receive potential safety information through the required process | Company routing procedure and owner |
| Compliance and privacy reviewers | Clarify requirements for engagement, content, and data use | Applicable rules and authorised reviewers |

This is a relationship map for discovery, based on the [role evidence](../../raw/MSL%20role%20and%20reporting%20research.md#role-and-relationships) and our [fictional engagement](../../raw/MSL%20KOL%20engagement%20note.md#an-illustrative-engagement-from-preparation-through-follow-up). It does not assign authority to named people.

### Training and scientific independence

MSLs generally have advanced scientific or clinical training. The professional statement describes medical, pharmacy, and scientific qualifications alongside communication and therapeutic expertise. [Scientific training evidence](../../raw/MSL%20scientific%20training%20and%20medical%20insights%20research.md#scientific-training).


## What an MSL does

The scientific exchange works in both directions:

- **Company to clinical community:** explain clinical evidence, study results, safety information, and scientific developments accurately and with balance.
- **Clinical community to company:** understand unanswered questions, evidence gaps, treatment challenges, and research needs.

MSL responsibilities include scientific exchange, support for evidence generation, and gathering field insights. [Role evidence](../../raw/MSL%20role%20and%20reporting%20research.md#role-and-relationships) and [discussion notes](../../raw/MSL%20KOL%20engagement%20note.md#the-msl-role-and-value).

**Hypothetical example:** an oncologist asks whether a trial's findings apply to patients with a particular coexisting condition. The MSL explains what the evidence supports, acknowledges its limitations, and captures the unanswered question for the relevant medical team. The MSL does not decide the patient's treatment. This example illustrates the role; it is not clinical advice or a recorded meeting.

## Why MSLs are useful to pharma companies

MSLs connect scientific evidence with questions arising in clinical practice. Their exchanges can reveal needs for further research, clearer scientific communication, or medical education. Credible exchange also supports research relationships and trust with external experts. [Role evidence](../../raw/MSL%20role%20and%20reporting%20research.md#role-and-relationships) and [discussion notes](../../raw/MSL%20KOL%20engagement%20note.md#the-msl-role-and-value).

For our analysis, the organisational value depends on whether a question or observation reaches a team that can assess it and respond. Recording a meeting alone does not demonstrate that an insight was used.

### Recording a conversation and capturing a potential insight

The following is a fictional comparison, not two statements from an actual KOL:

| Record type | Example | Meaning for analysis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meeting note | The doctor asked about elderly patients. | Records a topic, with limited context |
| Potential insight | The doctor identified limited evidence for frail elderly patients as an obstacle to interpreting the trial results. | Identifies a possible evidence gap and why it matters |

The potential insight is appropriate only if the doctor's actual remarks support it. A general question about age does not justify adding frailty or inferring an obstacle. Preserve the original statement, distinguish interpretation, and validate the meaning before sharing it as an insight. One comment does not establish a widespread pattern. See the [documentation example](../../raw/MSL%20KOL%20engagement%20note.md#documentation).

## Business problem

- **Current difficulty:** candidate problems include gathering relevant evidence, retaining meeting context, documenting accurately, and tracking questions to closure. These require validation with an MSL.
- **Affected users:** MSLs are the primary users; KOLs receive responses, and internal medical teams review questions and potential insights.
- **Evidence of the problem:** MAPS describes fragmented medical-insight practices and recommends clearer definitions, processes, responsibilities, and suitable technology. Its survey evidence is historical and does not validate problems in the intended company. [Medical insights evidence](../../raw/MSL%20scientific%20training%20and%20medical%20insights%20research.md#medical-insights-process).
- **Intended outcome:** proposed preparation and follow-up assistance that produces verifiable briefings, accurate records, and tasks with clear ownership and status. Scope remains subject to review.

### Operational and technical challenges

The table below contains hypotheses based on our [discussion notes, Operational and technical challenges](../../raw/MSL%20KOL%20engagement%20note.md#operational-and-technical-challenges). Company practices vary; these are not measured findings.

| Stage | Typical work | Operational difficulty to validate | Technical difficulty to validate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before the meeting | Review research, prior interactions, evidence, and open questions | Preparation effort and deciding what matters | Publications, interaction history, and reviewed content in separate systems |
| During the meeting | Discuss evidence, listen, clarify questions, and identify follow-up | Limited time and attention while capturing information | Preserving context and avoiding inaccurate summaries or inappropriate capture |
| After the meeting | Document, respond, and route relevant information | Administrative effort, unclear ownership, or delays | Reliable handoffs across CRM, medical-information, and safety processes |
| Across many meetings | Identify recurring evidence gaps for medical strategy | Distinguishing meaningful patterns from isolated opinions | Inconsistent notes, duplicate themes, and weak traceability |

MAPS supports treating insight management as an organisational process as well as a technology challenge. That does not establish that every row applies to this project. [Medical insights process](../../raw/MSL%20scientific%20training%20and%20medical%20insights%20research.md#medical-insights-process).
