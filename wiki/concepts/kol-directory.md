# Understanding KOLs and Maintaining an Expert Directory

For a pharma company, KOL identification starts with the therapy area and scientific need. This page explains who counts as a KOL, a working categorisation, where to find relevant experts, how an initial connection can be made, and how expert information connects with company interaction history.

## Who counts as a KOL

A Key Opinion Leader is an expert recognised for relevant scientific or clinical knowledge and influence. KOLs can be specialist doctors or scientific researchers. Being a doctor alone does not establish KOL status; wider Medical Affairs stakeholders are not automatically KOLs.

Relevant evidence includes expertise, publications, research activity, and contributions to professional societies or guidelines. Prescribing potential is not an appropriate basis for scientific expert selection. [Selection evidence](../../raw/KOL%20directory%20research%20notes.md#selection-and-first-contact).

Relevance is specific: a lung-cancer biomarker expert may be relevant to one evidence question but not another. Match expertise to the scientific purpose rather than relying on general prominence.

## How we categorise KOLs

This five-category framework summarises our discussion. Categories overlap and are not mutually exclusive.

| Category | Main contribution | Evidence to assess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research leaders | Generate evidence and contribute scientific expertise | Relevant studies, publications, and trial roles |
| Clinical experts | Understand treatment practice and evidence gaps | Specialist experience and clinical contributions |
| Guideline or society leaders | Contribute to recommendations and education | Guideline authorship and scientific committee roles |
| Emerging experts | Develop expertise through research and scientific contributions | Recent work, presentations, and growing collaboration |
| Digital opinion leaders | Communicate scientific perspectives through digital channels | Content quality, relevance, audience, and verifiable expertise |

The [Veeva PDF, pages 3 to 5](../../raw/Veeva%20four%20pillars%20of%20data-driven%20KOL%20engagement.pdf) distinguishes scientific, clinical, and digital leaders and discusses community and emerging experts. Our five categories regroup those ideas with professional selection criteria. Digital influence alone does not establish scientific authority.

Separately record therapy specialism, institution, geographic relevance, and local, national, or international reach. These are dimensions of a profile rather than additional mutually exclusive categories.

## Which drug categories relate to which experts

For discovery, therapy area is a practical starting point. The examples below are illustrative, not a market-share ranking, exhaustive classification, or formal ATC mapping.

| Therapy area | Examples of medicine categories | Relevant expertise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oncology | Anticancer therapies | Oncologists, haematologists, cancer researchers |
| Cardiovascular | Heart-failure, blood-pressure, lipid therapies | Cardiologists, cardiovascular researchers |
| Metabolic disease | Diabetes and obesity therapies | Endocrinologists, metabolic researchers |
| Immunology | Therapies for inflammatory or autoimmune disease | Rheumatologists, dermatologists, gastroenterologists |
| Neuroscience | Neurological and psychiatric therapies | Neurologists, psychiatrists, neuroscientists |
| Infectious disease | Antimicrobials and vaccines | Infectious-disease specialists, microbiologists, vaccine researchers |

Therapy area and medicine technology are different dimensions: biologics, for example, can span diseases. WHO ATC classifies medicines by organ system and therapeutic, pharmacological, and chemical properties; it does not prescribe which expert to engage. [Saved WHO reference](../../raw/WHO%20anatomical%20therapeutic%20chemical%20classification.html).

## Where companies can find each category

The following are candidate discovery routes. A listing or search result identifies a candidate; expertise and current affiliation still require verification.

| Category | Places to identify candidates |
| --- | --- |
| Research leaders | Publication authors, university or research-institute profiles, trial investigator listings |
| Clinical experts | Specialist hospital departments, referral centres, community practices, professional recommendations |
| Guideline or society leaders | Guideline author lists, professional-society committees, congress faculty |
| Emerging experts | Recent publications, conference presenters, trial and research teams |
| Digital opinion leaders | Professional scientific discussions and webinars, checked against institutional and research credentials |

Curated profiles combine multiple discovery signals. See [Expert profiles evidence](../../raw/KOL%20directory%20research%20notes.md#expert-profiles-and-identity) and [Veeva PDF, pages 3 to 6](../../raw/Veeva%20four%20pillars%20of%20data-driven%20KOL%20engagement.pdf).

Geographic relevance depends on the intended country, disease area, and research programme. There is no universal location ranking in these sources. International prominence and local relevance should be assessed separately.

## How a company makes the first connection

Professional guidance supports an introductory meeting to understand scientific needs and the expert's interest in engagement. [First-contact evidence](../../raw/KOL%20directory%20research%20notes.md#selection-and-first-contact).

Proposed outreach sequence for validation:

1. Identify the scientific purpose and verify that the expert is relevant.
2. Confirm current affiliation and an appropriate professional contact route.
3. Seek an introduction through a scientific contact, arrange a congress discussion, or make a professional meeting request through an authorised channel.
4. Explain the reason for contact and ask whether the topic is useful to the expert.
5. Agree the meeting purpose and capture follow-up in the company process.

Illustrative opening: "Your research on this topic relates to an evidence question we are exploring. Would you be interested in a scientific discussion?"

Public contact details do not automatically establish permission for every form of outreach. Channel use and data handling require applicable company rules and local requirements. Actual channels, owners, and permissions: [To complete].

## How the directory is maintained

Distinguish external scientific intelligence, the company identity record, and private interaction history.

| Layer | Information it contains | Example capabilities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| External expert intelligence | Publications, trials, affiliations, events, scientific and digital activity | Veeva Link Key People; IQVIA Expert Ecosystem |
| Company master directory | Consistent identity and institution links across systems | HCP master data; identifiers such as IQVIA OneKey |
| Medical interaction history | Meetings, questions, requests, follow-up status, recorded preferences | Company medical CRM |

Veeva and IQVIA describe linking expert intelligence to company systems. These examples do not establish which tools the intended company uses. [Profiles and identity evidence](../../raw/KOL%20directory%20research%20notes.md#expert-profiles-and-identity).

Proposed maintenance responsibilities:

- Use stable identifiers and check ambiguous names before linking publications or records.
- Verify affiliations and retain the source and date of verification.
- Review new scientific activity and reassess relevance to the current medical objective.
- Resolve duplicates while preserving provenance and interaction history.
- Record meetings and task updates through authorised company processes.
- Confirm data ownership, access, contact preferences, and applicable retention requirements.

An external directory identifies and profiles experts. The company CRM records its own relationship with them. An external provider would not automatically know the content of a private meeting. Actual maintenance owners, refresh schedules, integrations, and rules: [To complete].
